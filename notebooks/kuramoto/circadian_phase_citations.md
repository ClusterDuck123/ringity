# Circadian peak-time citations for `circadian_genes.json`

Each entry gives the primary-literature source for a gene's ZT peak-time assignment.
Where mRNA and protein peaks differ, both are noted.

---

### CCA1 — AT2G46830
**mRNA peak**: ZT0–2

Wang, Z.-Y. & Tobin, E.M. (1998). Constitutive expression of the CIRCADIAN CLOCK ASSOCIATED 1
(CCA1) gene and suppression of the circadian clock in Arabidopsis.
*Cell* **93**, 1207–1217. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(00)81464-6>

> "both CCA1 and LHY are expressed rhythmically with peaks of expression occurring soon after dawn"

---

### LHY — AT1G01060
**mRNA peak**: ZT0–2

Schaffer, R. et al. (1998). The late elongated hypocotyl mutation of Arabidopsis disrupts
circadian rhythms and the photoperiodic control of flowering.
*Cell* **93**, 1219–1229. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(00)81465-8>

> "LHY mRNA showed a circadian pattern of expression with a peak around dawn"

---

### LNK1 — AT5G64170 and LNK2 — AT3G54500
**mRNA peak**: ZT1.5–2

Rugnone, M.L. et al. (2013). LNK genes integrate light and clock signaling networks at the
periphery of the Arabidopsis circadian clock.
*PNAS* **110**, 12120–12125. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1300901110>

Xie, Q. et al. (2014). LNK1 and LNK2 are transcriptional coactivators in the Arabidopsis
circadian oscillator.
*Plant Cell* **26**, 2843–2857. PMC4145118.

> "peaks occurring in early morning (1.5 to 2 h after subjective dawn)"  (Rugnone 2013)

> "Expression of LNK1 and LNK2 cycles in phase with RVE4 and RVE8 and peaks in mid morning"  (Xie 2014)

---

### PRR7 — AT5G02810
**mRNA peak**: ZT4–8

Nakamichi, N. et al. (2010). PSEUDO-RESPONSE REGULATORS 9, 7, and 5 are transcriptional
repressors in the Arabidopsis circadian clock.
*Plant Cell* **22**, 594–605. PMC2861452. <https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.109.072892>

> "PRR9 mRNA levels are greatest at dawn, PRR7 peaks in the morning, PRR5 around noon,
> and PRR3 and TOC1 in the evening"

*Note*: Nakamichi 2010 gives relative descriptions ("morning"), not ZT numbers.
The range ZT4–8 is taken from the sequential PRR wave pattern established by
Matsushika et al. (2000) and Makino et al. (2000), where each PRR peaks ~2–4 h
after the previous one starting from PRR9 at ZT0.

---

### PRR5 — AT5G24470
**mRNA peak**: ZT8–10

Same source as PRR7 (Nakamichi 2010): *"PRR5 around noon."*

*Note*: "Noon" in a 12L:12D cycle is ZT6; Nakamichi's casual "noon" likely refers to
mid-light-period, consistent with the independently reported ZT8–10 peak in the PRR wave.

---

### PRR3 — AT5G60100
**mRNA peak**: ZT10–12

Same source as PRR7 (Nakamichi 2010): *"PRR3 and TOC1 in the evening."*

*Note*: Nakamichi groups PRR3 with TOC1 as "evening," but PRR3 peaks ~2 h before TOC1
in the sequential wave, placing it at ZT10–12.

---

### GI — AT1G22770
**mRNA peak**: ZT8–10

Fowler, S. et al. (1999). GIGANTEA: a circadian clock-controlled gene that regulates
photoperiodic flowering in Arabidopsis and encodes a protein with several possible
membrane-spanning domains.
*EMBO J* **18**, 4679–4688. <https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/18.17.4679>

> "a peak in transcript levels 8–10 hours after dawn"

**Protein peak**: ~ZT14–16 (GI protein accumulates in the afternoon and is stabilised
by ZTL interaction at dusk; David et al. 2006).

---

### RVE8 — AT3G09600
**mRNA peak**: ZT0 (same phase as CCA1/LHY)
**Protein peak**: ZT3–6
**EE DNA-binding activity**: ~ZT8

Rawat, R. et al. (2011). REVEILLE8 and PSEUDO-REPONSE REGULATOR5 form a negative feedback
loop within the Arabidopsis circadian clock.
*PLoS Genet* **7**, e1001350. PMC3069099. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1001350>

> "peak levels of RVE8-HA protein occurred three to six hours after subjective dawn (ZT27–ZT30)"

> "RVE8 protein levels are high in the subjective afternoon whereas CCA1 and LHY proteins
> are difficult to detect at that time"

Hsu, P.Y. et al. (2013). Accurate timekeeping is controlled by a cycling activator in Arabidopsis.
*eLife* **2**, e00473. PMC3639509. <https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.00473>

> "plants mutant for RVE8 and its two closest homologs, RVE4 and RVE6, have lost the
> afternoon-phased EE-binding activity"

---

### RVE4 — AT5G02840
**mRNA peak**: ZT0 (same phase as CCA1/LHY, by analogy with RVE8)
**Protein peak**: ZT3–8 (estimated; treated as functionally equivalent to RVE8)

Same sources as RVE8 above (Rawat 2011; Hsu 2013). Explicit ZT times for RVE4
alone are not reported; the triple rve4 rve6 rve8 mutant loses "afternoon-phased
EE-binding activity," placing RVE4 functional phase in the same window as RVE8.

---

### FKF1 — AT1G68050
**mRNA peak**: ~ZT8

Nelson, D.C. et al. (2000). FKF1, a clock-controlled gene that regulates the transition to
flowering in Arabidopsis.
*Cell* **101**, 331–340. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(00)80842-9>

Cited via Schultz, T.F. et al. (2001, PMC139480):

> "transcripts for FKF1 cycle robustly, with peak levels occurring at approximately 8 hr
> after dawn"

**Protein peak**: early evening (~ZT14–16); FKF1 protein is stabilised by light and
forms the GI–FKF1 complex at dusk (Imaizumi et al. 2003, *Science* 302:1407).

---

### CHE — AT5G08330
**mRNA peak**: ~ZT9 (9 h out of phase with CCA1)
**Protein peak**: ZT9

Pruneda-Paz, J.L. et al. (2009). A functional genomics approach reveals CHE as a component
of the Arabidopsis circadian clock.
*Science* **323**, 1481–1485. <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1167206>

> "CHE mRNA levels oscillate 9 hours out of phase with CCA1 transcript"

Kamioka, M. et al. (2016). Direct Repression of Evening Genes by CIRCADIAN CLOCK-ASSOCIATED1
in the Arabidopsis Circadian Clock.
*Plant Cell* **28**, 696–711. PMC4330950.

> "Both proteins are nuclear localized … reaching maximum levels at similar times of the
> day (ZT 9 and ZT13, respectively)"  [CHE at ZT9, TOC1 at ZT13]

---

### TOC1 — AT5G61380
**mRNA peak**: ZT12–14

Strayer, C. et al. (2000). Cloning of the Arabidopsis clock gene TOC1, an autoregulatory
response regulator homolog.
*Science* **289**, 768–771. <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.289.5480.768>

Also Nakamichi et al. (2010), same reference as PRR7: *"PRR3 and TOC1 in the evening."*

The Kamioka 2016 quote above places TOC1 protein at ZT13, consistent with ZT12–14 mRNA peak.

---

### ELF4 — AT2G40080
**mRNA peak**: ~ZT12

Doyle, M.R. et al. (2002). The ELF4 gene controls circadian rhythms and flowering time in
Arabidopsis thaliana.
*Nature* **419**, 74–77. <https://doi.org/10.1038/nature00954>

*Note*: Original paper paywalled; secondary sources consistently place ELF4 mRNA at dusk
(~ZT12), co-incident with ELF3–ELF4–LUX evening complex formation.

---

### ELF3 — AT2G25930
**mRNA peak**: ZT14–16

Liu, X.L. et al. (2001). ELF3 encodes a circadian clock-regulated nuclear protein that
functions in an Arabidopsis PHYB signal transduction pathway.
*Plant Cell* **13**, 1293–1304. PMC135582. <https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.13.6.1293>

> "ELF3 mRNA level is regulated in a cyclic manner, peaking at ~14 to 16 hr after sunrise
> regardless of daylength"

> "The maximal transcript level was observed ~16 hr after dawn in 12-hr-light/12-hr-dark
> cycles"

---

### ZTL — AT5G57360
**mRNA**: constitutive (no oscillation)
**Protein peak**: end of day / early night

Somers, D.E. et al. (2000). ZEITLUPE encodes a novel clock-associated PAS protein from
Arabidopsis.
*Cell* **101**, 319–329. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(00)80841-7>

> "ZTL transcript levels are constitutive"

Kim, W.-Y. et al. (2007). ZEITLUPE is a circadian photoreceptor stabilized by GIGANTEA in
blue light.
*Nature* **449**, 356–360. <https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06132>

> "ZTL protein levels are rhythmic with a peak in the late afternoon/early evening and
> trough at dawn, whereas ZTL mRNA levels are constitutive"

Moraes et al. (2015, PMC4348042): *"its protein abundance shows daily oscillation with a
peak at the end of the day."*

*Note*: "End of day" / "late afternoon/early evening" maps to ~ZT14–18.  The value ZT18
used in `PROTEIN_ZT` is on the late end of this range; ZT16 may be more accurate.

---

### LKP2 — AT2G18915
**mRNA**: constitutive (no oscillation)
**Protein peak**: uncharacterised

Schultz, T.F. et al. (2001). *Plant Cell* **13**, 2659–2670. PMC139480.
<https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.010354>

> "LKP2 is expressed at a low level and is not regulated by the circadian clock in
> Arabidopsis, similar to ZTL expression"

> "LKP2 transcript levels did not appear to change during the course of one LD cycle"

*Note*: LKP2 protein oscillation timing is not characterised in the primary literature.
The ZT20 value in `PROTEIN_ZT` is a placeholder by structural analogy to ZTL; it should
not be treated as a literature value.

## `circadian_genes_clean.json` — inclusion / exclusion reasoning

This file keeps only genes where the ZT peak assignment is grounded in a single, consistent
molecular layer (mRNA or protein) without large cross-layer disagreement.

### Included (clean)

| Gene | Used ZT | Why clean |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **CCA1** | ZT1 | mRNA, protein, and repressor function all peak at ZT0–2. |
| **LHY** | ZT1 | Same as CCA1. |
| **LNK1, LNK2** | ZT1.75 | mRNA peak ZT1.5–2, single well-characterised layer. |
| **PRR7** | ZT6 | PRR wave; mRNA, protein, and repressor activity consistent. |
| **PRR5** | ZT9 | PRR wave; mRNA ZT8–10, protein similar. |
| **PRR3** | ZT11 | PRR wave; mRNA ZT10–12, single bin boundary call. |
| **GI** | ZT9 | mRNA ZT8–10; protein ~2–4 h later (mild ambiguity). |
| **FKF1** | ZT8 | mRNA ~ZT8; protein active at dusk (mild ambiguity, same pattern as GI). |
| **CHE** | ZT9 | mRNA and protein both ZT9 — no cross-layer disagreement. |
| **TOC1** | ZT13 | mRNA ZT12–14, protein ZT13. Consistent. |
| **ELF3** | ZT15 | mRNA ZT14–16 regardless of photoperiod. |
| **ELF4** | ZT12 | mRNA ~ZT12 (dusk). |

### Excluded

| Gene | Reason for exclusion |
|------|----------------------|
| **RVE8** | mRNA ZT0, protein ZT3–6, DNA-binding activity ZT8 — spans 8 h across layers. |
| **RVE4** | Same mechanistic problem as RVE8. |
| **ZTL** | Constitutive mRNA; protein-stability peak only (~ZT14–18). |
| **LKP2** | Constitutive mRNA; protein oscillation uncharacterised. |
